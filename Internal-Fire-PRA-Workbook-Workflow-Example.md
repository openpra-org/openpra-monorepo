# Internal Fire PRA Workbook - High-Level Analyst Workflow

Here is the intended analyst workflow at a high level:

1. **Analysis Basis and At-Power Scope** - Define the intended application, capability category, at-power operating states, plant coverage, risk measures, and analysis boundaries.

2. **Evidence Base** - Collect and control the drawings, fire-protection records, equipment and cable data, procedures, operating experience, calculations, and plant-investigation evidence needed by the analysis.

3. **Baseline PRA and Technical Interfaces** - Establish the internal-events PRA models and controlled inputs received from other technical elements, along with the results Internal Fire PRA must provide downstream.

4. **Plant Boundary and PAU Partitioning** - Define the global analysis boundary and divide the plant into complete, nonoverlapping physical analysis units based on fire barriers, spatial separation, hazards, and potential damage.

5. **Fire Equipment Selection** - Identify initiating-event equipment, mitigating equipment, and instrumentation whose loss, spurious operation, or misleading indication could influence fire risk.

6. **Cable Selection and Location** - Identify the relevant cables, endpoints, raceways, and routing through each PAU, including overcurrent-protection concerns and justified routing assumptions.

7. **Qualitative Screening** - Apply consistent criteria to every PAU and retain any location capable of affecting modeled equipment, cables, plant trips, shutdown functions, or operator actions.

8. **Fire Plant Response Model** - Adapt the baseline PRA to represent fire-induced initiating events, equipment failures, spurious operations, success criteria, event sequences, systems logic, and supporting data.

9. **Fire Scenario Selection and Analysis** - Develop representative fire scenarios for retained PAUs and evaluate ignition sources, damage targets, fire growth, suppression, control-room abandonment, structural effects, and multi-compartment propagation.

10. **Fire Ignition Frequency** - Estimate ignition frequencies for retained PAUs and scenarios using applicable industry and plant-specific experience, including apportionment and uncertainty.

11. **Circuit Failure Analysis** - Determine how damaged cables and circuits can cause loss of function, hot shorts, spurious operation, or other modeled failure modes and quantify their probabilities.

12. **Fire Human Reliability Analysis** - Identify and quantify fire-specific operator actions and recovery actions while accounting for altered cues, smoke, access, timing, procedures, workload, and dependencies.

13. **Event Sequence Quantification and Uncertainty** - Combine ignition frequencies, fire-damage probabilities, circuit failures, human actions, and plant-response logic to calculate fire-induced sequence and sequence-family frequencies.

14. **Risk Interpretation** - Identify dominant PAUs, scenarios, ignition sources, equipment, cables, human actions, uncertainties, sensitivities, and potential model refinements.

15. **Risk Integration** - Transfer controlled Internal Fire results into overall PRA risk evaluation while addressing overlaps and dependencies with other hazards.

16. **Draft** - Assemble the complete analysis, calculations, assumptions, evidence, results, limitations, and requirement traceability into a controlled draft.

17. **Independent Review** - Perform technical and peer-review checks across every Internal Fire subelement and document findings and resolutions.

18. **Approval** - Confirm that review findings are resolved, conformance is demonstrated, and the approved model is placed under configuration control.
